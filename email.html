Stop chasing search volume

When Lashay Lewis first came on the pod a few years ago, she was preaching bottom-of-funnel content while most of us were still obsessed with traffic. Now she’s the founder of BOFU.ai, and she spends her days helping B2B SaaS companies get that same content found in AI search.

Which tells you something. Everyone is chasing a mythical AI playbook that will magically solve everything. But the fundamentals are what they’ve always been: real answers to the questions buyers actually have. AI search just rewards them more than ever.

So I went back to our conversation, and it holds up even better now than when we recorded it.

Sooner or later, every leader asks the big question: “How is this content making us money?” Most content teams scramble to answer it. Lashay has a clear answer.

🎯 Write for the 50, not the 50,000

Lashay isn’t trying to get in front of the most people. She wants to get in front of the people ready to make a decision: the buyer with a real problem who’s looking for an answer.

So instead of churning out 100 top-of-funnel articles in a 90-day sprint, go after the smaller, more specific searches from buyers who already know what they need to solve. They don’t need convincing. They need a solution, and you have one.

Back at Drift, we learned from customer research that sales leaders thought live chat just wasted their reps’ time on support questions. So we built content that laid out every one of those objections and answered them head-on. When you speak to the pain points your buyers actually have, converting them gets a lot easier.

👉 Lead with the pain point

At the bottom of the funnel, skip the generic stats. Your buyers have come this far already. Lead with the pain point, make it product-led, and focus on what actually matters to them. Lashay points to HubSpot and ServiceTitan as companies getting this right. Her advice: “Get to the point. Tell them why your solution is the best.” Then map specific features to specific pain points.

We broke down Lashay’s whole feature mapping framework in an earlier newsletter. If you missed it, it’s worth a read: How to create really good BOFU content.

Because in the middle of a busy day, with dozens of other deadlines and decisions, do I want to skim three paragraphs of filler before I get to what matters? Or do I want the info I need at the jump?

🔭 Be a sniper

If you’re on a small team with a lean budget, where do you want your time and energy to go? You can take the shotgun approach and cast a wide net with a slim chance of real impact. Or you can go the sniper route and zero in on the target that matters. And when it’s time to tie content to pipeline, bottom-of-funnel content is the easiest to connect to real results, with SQLs you can show within about 90 days.

It’s way too easy to get stuck on the hamster wheel of churning out top-of-funnel content. I’ve made that mistake myself. As marketers, we’re biased toward big numbers. It’s like playing the lotto, waiting for that one piece of content to pull in all the traffic and change my life. Bottom of the funnel is the safe money. It’s not as buzzy, but it gets the job done and meets the buyer where they are.

Start from the bottom. If I were leading a lean content team today (cue the “back in my day” and “content marketers these days” clichés), and we could only focus on one thing, I’d skip the top and go straight to the bottom of the funnel. You might miss out on some vanity metrics, but you’ll hit the ones that really matter. Paid teams have it easy: they can draw a straight line from the work to ROAS. Content teams have to prove their value, and the bottom of the funnel is where you can show it fastest and most directly.

But don’t just take it from me. Lashay has so much more great advice in the full episode.
